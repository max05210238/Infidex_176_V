# Focusing, Depth of Field, and Usage

Source: Infidex 176 V manual by Denis Aminev.

## Setting focus marks

You need ground glass to calibrate. The designer uses 1 mm microscope slide glass (75 x 25 mm) frosted on one side with glass matting paste.

1. Screw the helicoid fully in. On the Mamiya 80mm this is infinity, about **31 m (~102 ft)**.
2. Wrap a paper strip around the focus ring.
3. One **300 degree** turn covers infinity down to **2 m**. You can go closer into macro, but the lens can fully unscrew and fall out, so be careful.
4. Confirm infinity by focusing on something far away (a mountain, tower, or tall building) through the ground glass.
5. For near distances, place a bright, contrasty target at each distance (20, 15, 10, 5 m, and so on), focus via ground glass, and mark the strip.
6. Scan the finished strip and print your own focus scale.

A sample 300-degree strip is **170 mm** long. Typical marks: infinity, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2 m. Treat the sample as a guide, not an exact ruler. Calibrate to your own build.

Sharpest apertures sit in the middle of the range. f/16, f/22, and f/32 enter the diffraction zone. The table starts at 2 m because that matches how the designer uses the camera.

## First tests

You already verified infinity focus and helicoid travel during assembly. Next, test film transport.

Use a roll of already-developed, cheap, or expired 35mm film so a jam costs nothing. Simulate normal advance and rewind.

- **Crunching sound and torn sprocket holes:** the 8-tooth sprocket gear is too sharp and square. File it to a smoother profile. You can copy the tooth shape from another film camera.
- **Film slipping past gear teeth:** glue a piece of foam on the door near where the film contacts the sprocket gears to add tension.

## Shooting

- Format is 3:1, **72 x 24 mm**. One frame equals two standard 3:2 frames, exactly **16 sprockets**.
- A 36-exposure roll yields **18 frames** (19 if loaded in total darkness without wasting the leader, though the film may not lie as flat).
- Make sure the counter start mark is at the start position before shooting.
- Respooled films can hold fewer than 36 exposures.

**Frame counter quirk:** with the stock 8-tooth sprocket, you advance **2 revolutions of the frame counter per shot**. Swapping in a 16-tooth gear fixes this but lengthens the body, which the designer avoided.

## Viewfinder

The kit viewfinder (two variations) works by aligning both frame outlines into a single silhouette while looking through. It is reasonably accurate; keep headroom top and bottom. Alternatively use a 35mm optical viewfinder and mark its new framing using ground glass.

## Scanning

Long panoramic negatives are awkward for most labs. Options:

- **Flatbed:** Epson V850 Pro. Easiest, not the highest quality.
- **DSLR scanning:** photograph and stitch two frames. More work, better results.
