# Assembly Guide

Fourteen steps from printed parts to a shooting camera. Source: Infidex 176 V manual by Denis Aminev.

**Safety:** wear safety glasses and a respirator. Glue fumes are dangerous and will make your eyes water. Do not point a cutting knife toward yourself. Work without rushing.

## Before you glue anything

Check the fit of large parts first.

- Too loose: reprint.
- Too tight: shape with a file or sandpaper.
- Rotating parts should neither stick nor wobble.
- Exception: the taking spool shaft is meant to seat slightly deeper into its hole at the bottom of the body.

## Body

Unibody builders skip to the lens mount.

For two-piece bodies, a gap up to 0.4 mm between halves is acceptable. Sand the mating faces, clamp, and apply pressure while gluing. Fill the seam with putty or solder it.

**Critical:** keep the two frame planes as parallel as possible, because they define the focus plane. Place both halves on a flat surface, or use the film pressure plate sitting on the film rails as a guide while the glue sets.

If your body has brass-insert holes, install the inserts now with a soldering iron (M3 4.5x6 mm in front, 1/4" 8x8 mm in the base). Trim squeezed-out plastic flush.

## Lens mount

After the body is glued, dry-fit the lens mount into the ledge on the body. Grind material off the mount if needed.

**Step 1.** Glue the lens mount to the body with superglue or epoxy, or attach it temporarily with M3x10 screws. A small raised box on the part marks the bottom side. Apply glue to the indicated face, not the box.

> Tube mount and classical cone install the same way.

**Step 2.** Glue the lens board to the inner thread ring. Align the two highlighted marks so they sit on one line. Use the lower lens board only if the focal length is too long for infinity focus.

**Step 3.** Snap the fixation ring into the lens mount groove.

**Step 4.** Apply glue to the face of the fixation ring you just snapped in. **Do not glue it to the mount groove.** Place the focus ring on the fixation ring and press mildly for a few seconds. Do not put glue directly on the focus ring, or you risk bonding it to the mount. The focus ring must rotate freely without wobbling and without falling out of the groove.

**Step 5.** Fit the inner thread (with lens board) inside the focus ring so the mount slides drop into the inner-ring grooves. Rotate the focus ring clockwise. It should screw all the way in. If it binds, print a smaller inner thread or sand the surface. If greasing, use thick grease, not liquid. Unscrew it again afterward so it does not block the next steps.

## Film transport

**Step 6.** Place the spool base on the top side of the body. Apply glue to the marked area of the taking spool shaft, then use tweezers to insert it at a slight angle, push down and forward until it stops, and glue the two parts. It must still move freely up and down for now. Glue the winding knob on top later, after body and paint work.

**Step 7.** Drop the sprockets gear into its slot, then apply glue to the sprocket shaft and attach it to the gear through the shaft hole from above.

After painting, glue the frame counter cap to the sprocket shaft. Mask these top parts before painting, or fit them after paint.

## Rewind spring and door

**Step 8.** Bend the wire into a V-shaped spring with a small coil so it is stiff enough to hold the rewind knob. Measure and cut to length.

**Step 9.** Seat the spring inside its slot. There are three bumps that locate it. Insert the rewind knob and confirm it locks in both open and closed positions. Pull it back out afterward so it does not interfere with assembly and painting. This step is fiddly. Be patient.

**Step 10.** Place the door and check its fit. A slightly tight door is fine. With the door closed, hinge it by pushing the wire pin through the hole in the body below. Wire length is about 63 mm. It must fit tightly and not fall out in use. The hole can be soldered or puttied for security.

Cover the flat side of the pressure plate with black flocked paper or matte black vinyl. Do not use gray or lighter material, or you get halation and haze on images.

**Step 11.** Cut two foam strips, apply double-sided tape to both sides, and stick them to the pressure plate between the ribs. 4 to 5 mm thickness is enough.

Set the pressure plate flat inside the body where the film sits, so it covers the frame window without touching anything. Apply double-sided tape and close the door fully so the plate transfers onto the door. Open the door, press the plate against it a couple of times to seat it. Fit the latch and confirm the door sits flush with the body.

Attach the Mamiya lens to the inner thread ring using the original mount nut from the Mamiya TLR lens board.

## Finishing parts

**Step 12.** Mount a cold shoe (kit part or third-party) at your preferred spot.

**Step 13.** Mamiya TLR lenses lack a cable release port. Install the printed cable release port and lever knob. Print these at 0.1 to 0.12 mm layer height.

**Step 14.** Optionally print a lens hood (two variants, friction fit on the front element). Check your lens diameter and scale the hood to match.
